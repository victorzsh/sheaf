Why Sheaf

Built for people who live inside their PDFs

Not a viewer bolted onto Finder — a library that treats the documents themselves as the interface.

An infinite canvas

Every document is a stack of real pages, laid out at a size you can actually read. Zoom out for the whole library, zoom in to skim a page.

Folders with real color

Drag documents into folders that each get their own accent — so two folders with similar names still stand apart at a glance.

Synced through iCloud

Your library follows you between Macs. Documents download on demand, so a big archive doesn't have to live on every disk.

List and grid, too

Canvas is the everyday view — but renaming a dozen documents in a row, or hunting one by its cover, is a list or a grid away.

Search across every page

One shortcut finds text across the whole library, not just the document you have open.

Export .pdfx or a plain zip

Hand a library to someone who doesn't have Sheaf, or keep the whole thing portable as one archive.

Three ways to look

The same library, shaped for what you're doing

Canvas, list and grid all read off the same folders and documents — collapse one there, and it's collapsed everywhere.

Canvas
The everyday view — every document as a real, readable stack of pages.
List
One line per document — fast renames, fast reordering, fast scanning.
Grid
Covers first — for a library where the first page is the only label that matters.
